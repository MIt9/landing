The Long Read

By Dmytro Bilukha

Cornering a wrestler and leading a team of engineers turned out to be the same job.

I spent my twenties on a wrestling mat. Master of Sports in Greco-Roman, five regional titles, a bronze at the Ukrainian Universiade. Then I coached. Standing in the corner between periods, you get about twenty seconds to tell someone what to fix, in what order, and you have to sound certain while you do it.

Before IT, that was the job. Set the standard, drill the fundamentals, keep the group ahead of any single ego. When I switched to code in 2012 I thought I was starting from zero. I was not. The shape of the work was identical.

A decade later I run frontend for Silpo, the largest grocery retailer in Ukraine: three hundred stores, 900,000 organic visits a month, a storefront and a weekly promo circular that a lot of people rely on. I went from developer to Tech Lead in three years and have stayed at the same company for ten, because the interesting problems kept arriving faster than I could finish them.

I work from Vinnytsia, remote. Home is a wife, three kids, a dog, a cat and a fish. The mat taught me that fundamentals win, and that the person in the corner keeps their voice level. I have not found the part of engineering leadership where that stops being true.

Case Files

Two walkthroughs from the portfolio channel. Problem, fix, and the number that moved.

The last mile of accessibility is a person, not a score.

Problem
The automated accessibility score stalled at a point where screen-reader users still could not finish a shop.
Fix
Three months testing directly with a blind user. Fixed ARIA labels, focus order and live-region announcements in the header, cart and search that no audit catches.
Result
Audit score 60 to 95, and a batch of real usability bugs closed behind it.

No budget for the integration, so ship the smallest thing that works.

Problem
A full third-party integration was not funded. The obvious route was closed.
Fix
Shipped a minimal blind link, the least code that could route users through without the integration.
Result
Adoption moved from 0 to 10 percent.
